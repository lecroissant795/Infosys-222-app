# Multiple-Choice Question (MCQ) Quality Rules

## 1. Balanced Option Length (No "Longest Answer" Giveaway)
- **Avoid Length Bias**: The correct answer must NOT systematically be the longest or most detailed option. In a 4-option question, random chance is ~25%; the correct answer being the longest must not exceed ~25–35% across any chapter.
- **Detailed Distractors**: Distractors (incorrect options) must be written with the same depth, technical terminology, syntactic structure, and grammatical complexity as the correct answer.
- **Vary Lengths Intentionally**:
  - Frequently make one or more incorrect distractors the longest option.
  - Frequently make the correct answer concise and direct.
  - Keep all four options within a similar character length window (e.g., within ±20–30% of each other) whenever possible.

## 2. Plausible Distractors & Common Misconceptions
- Do not use obviously trivial, absurd, or filler distractors (e.g., "all computers shut down", "it deletes the entire internet").
- Ground distractors in authentic student misconceptions:
  - Confusing related normal forms (e.g., 2NF partial dependency vs 3NF transitive dependency).
  - Confusing SQL clauses (WHERE vs HAVING, DELETE vs TRUNCATE, UNION vs UNION ALL).
  - Confusing DBMS architecture layers or ACID properties (Isolation vs Consistency).
  - Confusing dimensional modeling patterns (Star vs Snowflake, Additive vs Semi-additive).

## 3. Position Balance
- Distribute the correct answer index evenly across all positions (A: 0, B: 1, C: 2, D: 3) so that test-takers cannot exploit positional bias.

## 4. Clear & Educational Explanations
- Every question must provide a clear explanation that explains:
  1. Why the correct option is right.
  2. Why the specific distractors are incorrect or what principle they violate.
